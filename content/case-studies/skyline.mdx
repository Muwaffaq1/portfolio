## The problem

Weather data is everywhere. The problem is weather apps that show too much at once, bury the forecast behind screens and use charts you have to interpret. Skyline was built from a written brief with one goal: speed of comprehension. A successful visit can last three to five seconds: open it, see 24°C and rain likely around 10 AM, take an umbrella, close it.

Everything follows one order: **now, next, later**. Now is the temperature, condition and high and low. Next is the coming hours. Later is the next five days. Anything outside that order has to earn its place.

## Only the card that matters

A rule engine picks which card to show, in priority order, so the screen never stacks five warnings:

<Table head={["Priority", "Card", "Shown when"]} rows={[["1", "Storm or heavy rain", "A storm, or a 70% chance of rain within 3 hours"], ["2", "Rain window", "Two or more hours in a row at 50% or more: \"Rain likely 4–6 PM\""], ["3", "UV", "UV index 6 or higher"], ["4", "Wind", "Wind of 35 km/h or more"], ["5", "Temperature drop", "A drop of 5° or more within 3 hours"], ["6", "Best time to be outside", "The nicest window in the next 12 hours"], ["7", "Compared with yesterday", "2° or more warmer or cooler than yesterday at this hour"], ["8", "Calm", "Nothing else applies"]]} />

A one-line brief under the city sums it up the same way: "Clear now, high of 29°."

## Tested where it matters

The rules people rely on have unit tests with Vitest: the card priorities, the rain window, the best time to be outside, the comparison with yesterday, the data clean-up and the sky logic. The code is public on [GitHub](https://github.com/Muwaffaq1/skyline).

## What shipped vs what was designed

Built and working as a web app with React, Vite and Open-Meteo, with a demo for every weather. It isn't published to the app stores, and there are no usage numbers.
